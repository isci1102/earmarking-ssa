# Results report: statutory revenue assignment in Sub-Saharan Africa

## Scope and interpretation

This report describes a **de jure statutory inventory**. The legal database records revenue instruments and allocation rules identified in selected legal corpora; it does not measure tax collections, cash transfers, disbursements, or expenditure execution. The Census is the universe identified in each selected corpus, not necessarily every revenue instrument operating in the country. Accordingly, `E/D` is always interpreted as the **share of current, non-penalty revenue instruments identified in the selected Census corpus that are linked to a statutory allocation**—not as the share of all taxes earmarked in a country.

Cross-country comparisons require an additional qualification. Seventeen countries are represented by a single-document Census, usually a consolidated tax register or similar legal source. Ghana and South Africa are multi-document pilots, based on 24 and 31 Census documents respectively (and 12 and 9 Allocation documents). Their observed allocation architecture is informative, but their Census counts and incidence ratios should not be mechanically ranked against countries covered by consolidated registers. [Table: 01_country_corpus_coverage.csv] [Figure: 04_africa_inventory_map.png] [Figure: 04_coverage_timeline.png]

The current stacking audit reports 19 Census and 19 Allocation files loaded, with no failed or duplicated country files. It also reports no missing analytical key identifiers, no cross-country links, and no ambiguous references. Of 1,473 flattened `census_ref` values, 1,459 (99.0%) matched within country; 13 were malformed and one was unmatched. The final expanded table removes six repeated links across four conflict-audited pairs—`BEN_PAIR_0001`, `CIV_PAIR_0090`, `CIV_PAIR_0091`, and `GIN_PAIR_0039`—and retains one authoritative link per `country + pair_id + census_instrument_id`. Of its 1,453 links, 1,327 match the current non-penalty Census; the other 126 point to instruments outside that analytical scope and are excluded from valid-link statistics rather than treated as linkage failures. :codex-annotation{index="1"} [Table: 01_stack_sanity_checks.xlsx, sheets `coverage`, `key_integrity`, and `link_filter_audit`] [Table: 02_country_summary.csv]

One saved file should not be used for the current analysis: `general/01_initial_descriptives.xlsx` is a legacy workbook reporting only 16 Census and 17 Allocation countries. It predates the current 19-country stack. The findings below therefore use the current `01_stack_sanity_checks.xlsx`, country summary, statistical CSVs, and plot-data CSVs. [Table: 01_initial_descriptives.xlsx, sheet `run_summary`] [Table: 01_stack_sanity_checks.xlsx, sheet `coverage`]

## Section 1 — The statutory allocation landscape in SSA

### Coverage, prevalence, and legal architecture

The current sample comprises 19 countries and 2,303 current, non-penalty Census instruments. Of these, 607 are linked to at least one statutory allocation. In pooled terms, this is 26.4% of the instruments in the selected analytical corpora, but that pooled ratio gives more weight to countries with larger corpora. The allocation layer contains 435 source groups, 803 distinct allocation pairs, and 1,327 valid atomic links between a Census instrument and an allocation pair. These counts show that statutory revenue assignment is an important part of the observed legal architecture; they do not measure the money raised or allocated. [Table: 02_country_summary.csv]

Statutory allocation is present throughout the sample but varies sharply across the selected corpora. Incidence ranges from 3.8% in the Democratic Republic of the Congo and 5.2% in Nigeria to 41.3% in Cameroon and 54.2% in Niger; Côte d’Ivoire, Mali, and Madagascar record 36.3%, 32.7%, and 31.4%, respectively. Ghana is at 22.7% and South Africa at 26.5%, but their multi-document pilot corpora make direct rankings against consolidated-register countries inappropriate. The data establish heterogeneity in the legal corpora reviewed; they do not establish that Niger allocates a larger share of national tax revenue than another country. [Table: 02_country_summary.csv] [Table: 04_earmark_incidence.csv] [Figure: 04_earmark_incidence.png]

The scale and complexity of allocation architecture also differ markedly. Côte d’Ivoire has 84 allocation-source groups, 183 pairs, and 339 atomic links; Niger has 62, 105, and 253. At the other end, the Democratic Republic of the Congo has two source groups, four pairs, and one valid atomic link, while Comoros has five of each. Consequently, pooled counts are influenced substantially by Côte d’Ivoire and Niger, as well as Mali, Cameroon, Chad, and the Republic of the Congo. [Table: 02_country_summary.csv]

Multiplicity is economically distinct from prevalence. The pooled data contain 1.85 allocation pairs per source group and 2.19 atomic links per linked Census instrument. Country-level atomic-link density ranges from 1.0 in Comoros and the Democratic Republic of the Congo to 3.0 in Nigeria, 2.77 in the Central African Republic, and 2.61 in Niger. Nigeria’s maximum is based on only three linked Census instruments and is therefore not a broad-sample benchmark. These ratios describe how many legal channels and Census links are attached to an instrument; they do not reveal what share of proceeds goes to each recipient. [Table: 02_country_summary.csv]

### Broad assignments combine earmarking and tax sharing

The broad allocation layer is not synonymous with functional earmarking. General tax sharing is the largest assignment category, with 357 pairs in 18 countries and a 43.4% equal-country average share. Functional earmarks account for 279 pairs in 17 countries and a 37.9% equal-country share. Both are therefore broad-based features of the observed legal architecture. However, assignment type is missing for 119 pairs across 12 countries—13.6% under equal-country weighting—so finer claims about the balance of assignment mechanisms should remain cautious. [Table: 03_assignment_type_summary.csv]

Proceeds-sharing channels dominate: 743 of 803 allocation pairs, present in all 19 countries, and 93.1% on an equal-country basis. Cost-recovery components account for 59 pairs in 11 countries and 6.6% on the same basis; allocation nature is missing for one pair. Purpose restrictions divide the broad layer more evenly: 473 unrestricted pairs are observed in all 19 countries, compared with 325 purpose-restricted pairs in 17 countries; their equal-country shares are 57.3% and 42.1%, with five pairs missing the restriction classification. The data thus show a mixed legal architecture of restricted assignments, unrestricted sharing, and some cost recovery—not a pure sample of earmarks. None of these counts indicates the fraction of actual revenue that is restricted. [Table: 03_allocation_nature_summary.csv] [Table: 03_purpose_restriction_summary.csv]

### Institutional routing is often subnational, but country models differ

Territorial governments are the largest beneficiary category in the broad layer: 306 pairs across 16 countries and a 39.9% equal-country average share. Funds account for 150 pairs across 17 countries and 24.4%; agencies for 141 pairs across 15 countries and 16.5%; and general budgets for 101 pairs across 16 countries and 11.6%. Beneficiary type is missing for six pairs across four countries. The agreement between pooled counts and equal-country averages indicates that subnational routing is not merely a feature of one high-count country. Economically, this reflects extensive statutory vertical sharing alongside routing through dedicated public bodies. [Table: 03_beneficiary_type_summary.csv] [Table: 04_beneficiary_equal_country.csv] [Figure: 04_beneficiary_equal_country.png]

The institutional models nevertheless differ substantially. Territorial governments receive 83.3% of allocation pairs in Senegal, 79.3% in Mali, 78.6% in Guinea, and 75.0% in the Democratic Republic of the Congo. By contrast, funds receive 84.0% in Ghana and 52.9% in South Africa; agencies are the largest category in Côte d’Ivoire and the Republic of the Congo; and the general budget is Togo’s largest category. These are informative differences in statutory routing. They do not show whether funds or subnational governments actually received or spent the amounts specified in law. [Table: 04_beneficiary_country_composition.csv] [Figure: 04_beneficiary_country_composition.png]

### Broad bases coexist with sector-specific charges

Among the 607 linked valid Census instruments, general-economy-wide instruments are the largest source category, with 102 instruments and a 29.5% equal-country average share. Real estate follows under equal-country weighting at 11.9%, then mining and quarrying at 9.0%, transport and storage at 8.1%, manufacturing at 7.7%, and trade at 5.4%; six linked instruments have no base-sector classification. Transport is second in the pooled count, with 86 instruments, but only fourth under equal-country weighting, suggesting that part of its pooled prominence comes from higher-count countries. General-economy-wide instruments rank first under both measures, making that pattern more broad-based. These are statutory source-base classifications; they are not estimates of the ultimate economic incidence of the charge. [Table: 04_base_sector_pooled.csv] [Table: 04_base_sector_equal_country.csv] [Figure: 04_base_sector_pooled.png] [Figure: 04_base_sector_equal_country.png]

### Destinations reveal both intergovernmental sharing and functional assignment

Local budgets are the leading broad destination, with 298 distinct pair–division links. Economic affairs follows with 140, general public services with 115, and the general budget with 104. Under equal-country weighting, the corresponding shares of within-country pair–function links are 36.8%, 15.3%, 12.3%, and 11.8%. Local-budget routing therefore remains prominent after giving every country equal weight. One allocation pair can support several functions, so destination-link counts can exceed the 803 allocation pairs. [Table: 04_destination_pooled_division.csv] [Table: 04_destination_equal_country_division.csv] [Figure: 04_destination_pooled_division.png] [Figure: 04_destination_equal_country_division.png]

At the detailed function level, executive, legislative, fiscal, and external affairs is the leading substantive category, with 87 links across 14 countries. Transport has 37 links across six countries; agriculture, forestry, fishing, and hunting has 24 across eight; and general transfers between government levels has 22 across six. All observed codes used in the destination tables are either mapped GFSM functions or explicitly preserved non-GFSM categories; none is silently dropped as unmapped. These counts illustrate the range of statutory purposes, while the country counts show that no single detailed function is universal. [Table: 03_destination_function_summary.csv] [Table: 04_destination_pooled_cofog4.csv] [Figure: 04_destination_pooled_cofog4.png]

Country destination profiles are highly concentrated in some cases and diversified in others. Local budgets are the largest broad destination in 10 of 19 countries, accounting for 83.3% of non-unallocated destination links in Mali and Senegal, 78.6% in Guinea, 75.0% in the Democratic Republic of the Congo, and 65.2% in Madagascar. Economic affairs leads in Ghana (56.0%), Nigeria (40.0%), the Republic of the Congo (35.6%), and Côte d’Ivoire (27.5%); social protection leads in South Africa (35.3%). The largest destination ranges from only 25.0% in the Central African Republic to 83.3% in Mali and Senegal. The observed architecture is therefore neither uniformly subnational nor uniformly sectoral. [Table: 04_destination_largest_division.csv] [Table: 04_destination_country_heatmap_division.csv] [Figure: 04_destination_largest_division.png] [Figure: 04_destination_country_heatmap_division.png]

Source and destination are not one-to-one. Of 248 atomic destination links originating in general-economy-wide instruments, 98 (39.5%) go to local budgets. Real-estate sources route 60 of 114 links (52.6%) to local budgets. Transport and storage is more diversified: 59 of 199 links (29.6%) go to economic affairs, 55 (27.6%) to local budgets, 51 (25.6%) to the general budget, and 22 (11.1%) to general public services. Public-administration sources are more concentrated, with 48 of 79 links (60.8%) going to general public services. These pooled relationships describe statutory routing and may be influenced by high-count countries; they do not establish benefit incidence or a causal link between the charged sector and spending. [Table: 04_source_destination_heatmap_division.csv] [Figure: 04_source_destination_heatmap_division.png]

## Section 2 — Partial and exclusive earmarks

### The functional-instrument restriction removes roughly half of the broad layer

The restricted results retain source instruments classified as either `partial_earmark` or `exclusive_earmark`. An exclusive earmark has only purpose-restricted proceeds-allocation channels among its observed proceeds channels; a partial earmark combines restricted and unrestricted proceeds-allocation channels. The filter is instrument-level, so all observed channels of a qualifying source instrument remain—including unrestricted or cost-recovery channels attached to partial-earmark instruments. The classification therefore describes legal channel architecture, not the fraction of actual receipts earmarked.

The filtered layer retains 433 of 803 allocation pairs, a 46.1% reduction; 219 of 435 source groups, a 49.7% reduction; 694 of 1,327 valid atomic links, a 47.7% reduction; and 329 of 607 linked valid Census instruments, a 45.8% reduction. Seventeen of the 19 allocation countries remain; the Democratic Republic of the Congo and Senegal have no qualifying pairs. Roughly half of the observed statutory-assignment architecture is thus attached to instruments that combine or exclusively use restricted proceeds channels. This is a count-based statement, not a revenue-weighted one. [Table: general/02_country_summary.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/02_country_summary.csv]

The contraction is highly uneven. Ghana retains 24 of 25 pairs (96.0%), Nigeria 8 of 9 (88.9%), South Africa 15 of 17 (88.2%), the Central African Republic 13 of 15 (86.7%), and Côte d’Ivoire 153 of 183 (83.6%). By contrast, Mali retains 10 of 82 (12.2%), Guinea 4 of 28 (14.3%), Niger 17 of 105 (16.2%), Benin 7 of 22 (31.8%), and Madagascar 21 of 48 (43.8%). The selected-corpus incidence measure consequently falls from 54.2% to 9.5% in Niger, from 32.7% to 4.0% in Mali, from 41.3% to 18.7% in Cameroon, and from 31.4% to 9.3% in Madagascar. The relatively high retention in Ghana and South Africa is informative pilot evidence about their observed allocation architecture, not a basis for ranking national prevalence. [Table: general/02_country_summary.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/02_country_summary.csv] [Figure: general/04_earmark_incidence.png] [Figure: partial_exclusive/04_earmark_incidence.png]

### The filtered architecture is more purpose-oriented

Functional-earmark pairs barely change, from 279 to 278, while their equal-country share rises from 37.9% to 70.3%. General tax-sharing pairs fall from 357 to 72 and their equal-country share from 43.4% to 11.7%. Purpose-restricted pairs decline only from 325 to 314, retaining 96.6%, whereas unrestricted pairs fall from 473 to 119, retaining 25.2%. The equal-country restricted share consequently rises from 42.1% to 77.1%. These shifts show that the instrument filter isolates a clearer purpose-oriented architecture, while the remaining 119 unrestricted channels are expected because partial-earmark instruments retain their unrestricted channels. [Table: general/03_assignment_type_summary.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/03_assignment_type_summary.csv] [Table: general/03_purpose_restriction_summary.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/03_purpose_restriction_summary.csv]

Proceeds-sharing remains dominant, rising from 93.1% to 97.6% under equal-country weighting. Seventeen cost-recovery components remain in five countries because qualifying instruments can also contain such channels. This reinforces that the restricted sample is an instrument-level subset rather than a row-level table containing only strictly restricted proceeds channels. [Table: general/03_allocation_nature_summary.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/03_allocation_nature_summary.csv]

### Institutional routing shifts toward funds and agencies

The most pronounced beneficiary change is away from territorial governments and toward dedicated public bodies. The equal-country share routed to funds rises from 24.4% to 45.6%, and the agency share from 16.5% to 22.6%; the territorial-government share falls from 39.9% to 8.3%. Programs rise from 3.4% to 6.9%, while the general-budget share rises from 11.6% to 14.4%. The fund share increases in 16 of the 17 remaining countries, while territorial-government shares fall in 14 and rise in none. The pattern is therefore broad-based rather than driven solely by Côte d’Ivoire’s large number of pairs. Economically, functional earmark instruments are more commonly routed through special-purpose funds and public bodies than through general territorial sharing. [Table: general/04_beneficiary_equal_country.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/04_beneficiary_equal_country.csv] [Table: general/04_beneficiary_country_composition.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/04_beneficiary_country_composition.csv] [Figure: general/04_beneficiary_equal_country.png] [Figure: partial_exclusive/04_beneficiary_equal_country.png] [Figure: partial_exclusive/04_beneficiary_country_composition.png]

### Charged sectors and destinations become more function-specific

Under equal-country weighting, mining and quarrying rises from 9.0% to 15.3% of linked Census instruments and manufacturing from 7.7% to 14.5%. General-economy-wide instruments remain the largest source category but decline from 29.5% to 25.3%; real estate falls from 11.9% to 4.6%. In pooled filtered counts, the leading sources are general-economy-wide instruments (52), transport and storage (50), mining and quarrying (42), and manufacturing (33). Because the shift is visible under equal-country weighting, it is not solely the result of countries with larger inventories. It still measures instrument counts rather than revenue yield or economic tax incidence. [Table: general/04_base_sector_equal_country.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/04_base_sector_equal_country.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/04_base_sector_pooled.csv] [Figure: partial_exclusive/04_base_sector_equal_country.png] [Figure: partial_exclusive/04_base_sector_pooled.png]

Destination composition changes even more sharply. Local-budget links fall from 298 to 36, and their equal-country share from 36.8% to 5.7%. Economic-affairs links remain almost intact—133 of the original 140—and their equal-country share rises from 15.3% to 28.9%. General-public-services links fall from 115 to 53 and general-budget links from 104 to 71, while all 39 recreation, culture, and religion links and all 31 environmental-protection links remain. Economic affairs is the largest destination in eight of the 17 remaining countries, although Côte d’Ivoire contributes 50 of the 133 pooled links. The data show that identifiable functional destinations replace much of the unrestricted local-budget routing in this subset; they do not show actual spending by function. [Table: general/04_destination_pooled_division.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/04_destination_pooled_division.csv] [Table: general/04_destination_equal_country_division.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/04_destination_equal_country_division.csv] [Table: partial_exclusive/04_destination_largest_division.csv] [Figure: partial_exclusive/04_destination_pooled_division.png] [Figure: partial_exclusive/04_destination_equal_country_division.png] [Figure: partial_exclusive/04_destination_largest_division.png]

The source-to-destination matrix suggests closer descriptive alignment for several charged sectors. Economic affairs receives 51 of 108 transport-source links (47.2%), 22 of 42 agriculture-source links (52.4%), and 31 of 85 mining-source links (36.5%). Transport also routes 39 links (36.1%) to the general budget, while real-estate sources retain 16 of 46 links (34.8%) to local budgets. These are pooled associations and may be affected by countries with many links; they are not strong enough to support a universal source–destination relationship or any causal interpretation. [Table: partial_exclusive/04_source_destination_heatmap_division.csv] [Figure: partial_exclusive/04_source_destination_heatmap_division.png]

The saved partial/exclusive tables pool the two statuses and do not retain an `earmark_status` breakdown. A separate comparison of partial versus exclusive instruments is therefore **not supported by the permitted saved outputs** and should not feature in a presentation. In particular, the combined results cannot be used to infer how prevalent exclusive earmarks are relative to partial earmarks.

## Section 3 — Ghana earmarked-fund budgets

### The broad assigned-fund envelope is large but not monotonic as a budget share

The annual budget allocated to all identified earmarked or assigned funds rises from GH¢9.73 billion in 2017 to GH¢63.55 billion in 2026, a 6.53-fold nominal increase. Its share of total government expenditure moves from 17.9% to 21.0%, with a low of 15.7% in 2023. The data therefore show rapid nominal growth but a much narrower, non-monotonic range of relative budget weight. These are budget or appropriation measures represented in the source tables—not tax collections, actual transfers, or executed expenditure. [Table: 06_ghana_all_earmarked_transfers_amount.csv] [Figure: 06_ghana_all_earmarked_transfers_amount.png] [Table: 06_ghana_all_earmarked_transfers_share.csv] [Figure: 06_ghana_all_earmarked_transfers_share.png]

### The exclusive-earmark component is smaller and more volatile

Across the seven funds linked to instruments classified as exclusive earmarks, the aggregate budget increases from GH¢4.17 billion in 2018 to GH¢26.88 billion in 2026. Its share of total expenditure declines from 6.8% in 2018 to 4.5% in 2021 and a low of 3.4% in 2023, before rising to 5.7% in 2024, 8.7% in 2025, and 8.9% in 2026. Nominal amounts also decline in 2021 and 2023 before increasing strongly thereafter. The evidence describes changing budget provision for these funds; it cannot identify the policy or macroeconomic forces behind the movements. [Table: 06_ghana_total_exclusive_fund_amount.csv] [Figure: 06_ghana_total_exclusive_fund_amount.png] [Table: 06_ghana_total_exclusive_fund_share.csv] [Figure: 06_ghana_total_exclusive_fund_share.png]

The exclusive subset represents 34.6% of the broad assigned-fund amount in 2018, falls to 21.5% in 2023, and reaches 42.3% in 2026. From 2023 to 2026, the broad expenditure share rises by 5.3 percentage points, from 15.7% to 21.0%, while the exclusive subset rises by 5.5 points, from 3.4% to 8.9%. Arithmetically, the recent increase in the broad envelope is concentrated in the exclusive-earmark funds; this comparison does not explain why the appropriations changed or whether they were executed. The Ghana Education Trust Fund is consistently second and rises from 22.2% to 36.7%. Together, the two funds increase from 65.7% to 78.7% of the exclusive aggregate. In 2026, their combined appropriation is GH¢21.17 billion—GH¢11.30 billion for health and GH¢9.87 billion for education—equivalent to about 7.0% of total government expenditure. This is concentration in budget provision, not evidence of effective sectoral spending. ## Main stylized facts

### 1. Statutory revenue assignment is widespread but highly heterogeneous across the observed legal corpora.

Incidence ranges from 3.8% in the Democratic Republic of the Congo to 54.2% in Niger; the pooled database links 607 of 2,303 current, non-penalty Census instruments. [Table: general/02_country_summary.csv] [Figure: general/04_earmark_incidence.png]

**Interpretation:** statutory allocation is a material feature of the legal systems reviewed, but its prevalence and architecture vary greatly. **Main caveat:** these are selected-corpus instrument counts, not national revenue shares, and Ghana and South Africa are not mechanically comparable with consolidated-register countries.

### 2. The broad allocation layer combines functional earmarking with substantial unrestricted tax sharing.
