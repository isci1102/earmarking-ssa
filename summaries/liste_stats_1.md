Below is the operational build sheet. I treat the **global preliminary statistics as “Table 0”**, separate from the ten analytical exhibits.

The conventions follow the database methodology: the census provides the instrument denominator; the allocation table contains canonical statutory channels identified by `pair_id`; the strict functional-earmark filter is `allocation_nature == "proceeds_share"` and `is_purpose_restricted == 1`; and the database measures legal assignments rather than revenue amounts or execution.  The exhibits are designed to connect the database to the policy mechanisms highlighted in the proposal—tax acceptance, transparency, priority funding, flexibility, and fiscal-management complexity—without claiming that the database identifies those outcomes. 

## Common analytical conventions

| Label | Baseline definition |
|---|---|
| **Census baseline** | Distinct country census `instrument_id`; current (`in_force_reconciled == 1`, otherwise `in_force == 1`);  `is_penalty == 0`. |
| **Allocation baseline** | `row_type == "allocation"`; `pair_row_role != "restatement"`; distinct `(country, pair_id)`. |
| **Strict earmark allocation** | `allocation_nature == "proceeds_share"` and `is_purpose_restricted == 1`. |
| **Any statutory routing** | At least one canonical allocation row, whether functional earmark, tax sharing, equalization, derivation, cost recovery, general-budget residual, or supranational assignment. |
| **Census join** | Flatten `census_ref`; map every reference to the surviving country census `instrument_id`; deduplicate before counting instruments. Never count the length of `census_ref` directly. |
| **Regional summary** | First calculate each statistic separately for each country. Report the unweighted mean and median across the 19 countries. Pooled instrument/channel statistics are secondary. |
| **Interpretation** | Say “among the 19 covered countries,” not “in SSA.” Counts are instruments or legal channels, not revenue amounts. |
| **Priority codes** | **H:** headline; **E:** essential main text; **S:** supporting/mergeable; **M:** mandatory preliminary or disclosure; **C:** conditional on data comparability; **A:** appendix. |

## Space-constrained hierarchy

The two exhibits most likely to change an IMF reader’s understanding are:

1. **Exhibit 5: the source–destination matrix**, because it shows what parts of the revenue system are legally connected to what functions.
2. **Exhibit 7: prevalence versus routing complexity**, because it demonstrates that two countries with the same earmarking prevalence can face entirely different institutional and fiscal-management issues.

Immediately behind them is **Exhibit 3**, because the distinction between a small top-slice and full dedication is central to the potential constraint on budget flexibility.

With room for only eight exhibits, combine **Exhibits 4 and 6** into one institutional-routing exhibit and place **Ghana/Exhibit 10** in a boxed case. Raw country counts, language-group comparisons, choropleth maps, composite earmarking rankings, and cross-country regression tables should not receive main-text space.
