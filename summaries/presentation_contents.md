### BAND 1 — §8 | SSA CONDITIONS

**The SSA material supports a demand–enforceability tension in which earmarking is sought where trust and ordinary budgeting are weak, while its claimed mechanisms require credible commitment, participation, administration, and oversight; the corpus does not establish this as a universal SSA result.**

**Institutional requirements.** Lee and Wagner’s normative analysis requires a strong and broadly uniform taxed-good/financed-good relationship and constitutional protection against fiscal discrimination; Allen and Radev’s institutional review requires effective central budgeting, trained staff, information systems, audit, reporting, tax administration, and cash management; Gwilliam and Shalizi’s African road-fund review identifies an intermediate governance window in which ordinary budgeting is deficient but user governance, audit, and protection against diversion remain credible. (Lee and Wagner, ~1991, pp. 118, 122–123; Allen and Radev, 2006, pp. 14–16, 21–24; Gwilliam and Shalizi, 1999; §8.1)

**SSA observations.** Prichard’s Ghana process evidence reports adoption of VAT-linked education and health earmarks amid politically salient resistance, while the Communications Tax political claim exceeded the statutory dedication and early aggregate additionality coexisted with non-wage-composition and governance qualifications. McCleary’s five-country road-fund review included four SSA cases—Central African Republic, Ghana, Mali, and Zaire—and gave only Ghana a partly favorable assessment, itself qualified by an independent policy commitment. Cashin, Sparkes, and Bloom report an eventual Ghana NHIS bailout, unused Botswana tobacco resources where the transfer mechanism was missing, and South African review arrangements that revised some earmarks while carrying others forward. (Prichard, 2015, pp. 92–104; McCleary, 1991, pp. 90–92; Cashin, Sparkes, and Bloom, 2017, Ghana/Botswana/South Africa cases; §8.2)

**Identification condition.** Teja, Bird and Jun, and McCleary each treat weak or cumbersome ordinary budgeting as a possible reason earmarks are adopted; the evidence does not identify whether institutional weakness precedes adoption, predicts later failure, or jointly shapes both. (Teja, 1988, pp. 14–19; Bird and Jun, 2005, pp. ~13–14; McCleary, 1991; §8.3)

### Slide 5 | PART 3 — FRAMEWORK

**Title:** Selection favors the minimum passage-sufficient earmark

**Subtitle:** Read the model as a choice among no tax, ordinary financing, and statutory dedication.

**Content:**

1. The proposal’s rate, base, administrative design, expected gross revenue, and earmarked destination are fixed; the government chooses only whether to introduce the tax and what share of its proceeds to earmark.
2. (M_{NE}) is the passage position without earmarking; (G_E) is the support difference between full and zero earmarking; (V_T) is the tax’s fiscal value under ordinary allocation; (L_E) is the fiscal-flexibility loss under full earmarking.
3. When the non-earmarked proposal already meets the passage threshold and its fiscal value is nonnegative, the model selects the non-earmarked tax.
4. When the non-earmarked proposal is blocked, full earmarking covers the support shortfall, and fiscal value covers the required restriction, the selected share is the smallest passage-sufficient share:

[
\alpha^{*}=-\frac{M_{NE}}{G_E}
]

5. When either political feasibility or fiscal value is insufficient, the model selects no tax.

**Source:**

1. `conceptual_framework_draft.pdf` §§1.1–1.3.
2. `conceptual_framework_draft.pdf` §§1.2–1.3.
3. `conceptual_framework_draft.pdf` §1.4.
4. `conceptual_framework_draft.pdf` §§1.4–1.5.
5. `conceptual_framework_draft.pdf` §1.4. 

**Visual:** equation — place the single equation at the center of a three-branch decision structure: non-earmarked tax / minimum earmark / no tax.

**Footer:** Does not model expenditure execution, welfare, additionality, or taxpayer response.

**Why:** This is the framework’s central selection result and the link to observed statutory shares.

### Slide 13 | PART 5 — METHODOLOGY

2. Instrument identity uses normalized name, taxed base, payer, and explicit legal-continuity signals; name similarity alone is not decisive.
3. Pair identity uses instrument, normalized recipient, allocation level, parent pool, and base scope; routing accounts are treated as routes rather than additional destinations.
4. Where several statements describe the same channel, the governing row is selected through share completeness, key completeness, share basis, and source precedence; the remaining statements stay available as restatements.
5. A source-only instrument remains in reconciled evidence; a missing destination may sit in an unavailable subordinate text and is not coded as proof of no assignment.
6. Repeal, replacement, joint keys, competing shares, competing rates, and stated inconsistencies receive explicit lineage or conflict fields.

### Slide 14 | PART 5 — METHODOLOGY

**Title:** Audit rules remain explicit and bounded

**Subtitle:** Pair the audit machinery with current stack diagnostics and the stated recall limit.

**Content:**

2. **LOCATE reconciliation:** every inventory entry must be either extracted or dismissed with a recorded reason; passages newly recovered by EXTRACT require a logged amendment to the LOCATE inventory.
3. **Human-review worklist:** low or medium confidence, boundary calls, conflicts, partial keys, nested shares, inferred or unallocated functions, and unmatched or tied census links.
4. **Current stack:** 19 Census files and 19 Allocation files loaded, with no failed or duplicated country files, missing analytical keys, cross-country links, or ambiguous references.
5. **Link audit:** 1,459 of 1,473 flattened `census_ref` values matched within country, or 99.0%; 13 were malformed and one was unmatched.
